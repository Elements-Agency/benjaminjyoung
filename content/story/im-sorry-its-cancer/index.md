Artistic mastery requires an unyielding commitment to truth—even when that truth arrives in a single, life-altering sentence.

<!--more-->

Painted in the quiet stillness following my diagnosis in March 2026, I'm Sorry, It's Cancer represents a pivotal moment in my body of work. Executed with meticulous drybrush watercolor technique on archival cotton paper, every stroke captures the suspended gravity of a fleeting second—and the resolute strength that immediately followed.

This piece is not a document of illness; it is a declaration of enduring life, narrative persistence, and the relentless pursuit of beauty through realism.

A moment that will resonate with others familiar with that feeling, and a feeling that would not last long, because I'm not ready to give up.
