Hello Art Collector,

I have a lot of amazing things to cover for this month's artist newsletter. Before anything else, I need to talk about this most important piece of news. If you have been following my work and story at all, you will know I have been in a fight against cancer for for some time now. It's honestly been an emotional rollercoaster, and after treatment, it was coming down to the wire finding out if I was in the clear or if a biopsied suspicious lymph node meant my cancer had spread.

<!--more-->

**It's official... My cancer is in remission!**

I honestly was not expecting the amount of relief I felt finding this out. Perhaps, I'm not the best of being aware of how heavy things can weigh on me. Well... I should know that by now, with my past, but we'll talk about that later.

## "Darkness to Light" & The 2026 Door 2 Art Show

Finding out this great news, makes the next great news even more appreciative for me:

This past weekend was the opening and reception for the [2026 Door 2 Art Show](https://www.door2art.com/2026-award-winners). This exhibit is the biggest crowd I get to see with any of the exhibits I have visited before. A judged exhibit I submitted only one painting to, ["I'm Sorry, It's Cancer"](https://benjaminjyoung.com/story/im-sorry-its-cancer/). This watercolor self-portrait was to capture the feeling of the moment, that I first heard my diagnosis. This year was a first, where they included a theme artists submitting their work could opt to be apart of. The theme being "Darkness to Light", which I think is a perfect fit for my entire narrative now and in the future, and especially for this piece especially.

Even better news...

**"I'm Sorry, It's Cancer" was awarded 1st place!**

## Opening Up Commission Requests

Since I have more time, and the hopes that I can potentially transition to a full time artist, I have decided to finally start taking commissions again. I know I have had a lot of requests in the past for this, and I have turned down a lot of you. But if you're still considering a watercolor painting by me, now is the time.

If I get enough commission requests, then I will make the decision to go full time. It's that last little push I need to get me there. For now, my commission prices will be starting at $5 per square inch. So that means a 12x16 inch painting, which is 192 square inches, will cost $960 for a commission. If you're local, this will include basic framing, or shipping rolled up in a protective tube if you are not. A 50% deposit will required before starting, and the rest due upon completion.

## Stark County Artists Exhibition at Massillon Museum

Now that the Door 2 Art show is over, and after it's done being display at the Winner's Circle showing, it will be off to it's next exhibit. **The 2026 Stark County Artists Exhibit at the Massillon Museum!**

{{< row-start >}}
    {{< img col="8" src="crowd.jpg" alt="A crowd viewing artwork at Door 2 Art" title="I'm Sorry, It's Cancer" >}}
{{< row-end >}}

Even though Massillon has a notorious reputation of their artist community being unwelcoming or unkind, even threateningly hostile at times to outsiders, it is however taking me back to a city I lived in for many years. Prior to my short-lived move to Germany, Massillon is the city where many of the events of my current and future works unfolded. And of course the start of the events that eventually lead me in becoming an artist. Which one day I will get in to with narrative works.

Being featured in Massillon Museum is always a great honor. Especially in a Museum I used to belong as a patron to their James & Eliza Duncan Society, years before I even considered drawing or painting again. It makes it especially meaningful to now be one of the artists in an museum I used to admire the artwork in.

The exhibit will run from November 7th through January 10th. You can catch me at the reception for the exhibit, accompanied with the lovely Miss Dianne, who I'm sure will be keeping me smiling.

## On the Easel: Light, Shadow, and the Road Ahead

I'm still trying to finish up a gift for Dianne, a watercolor painting for **Cherry Blossoms**. After that, it's time to get back to painting narratives that will go in to a very dark place. Here is that nice light work in progress, before we start talking about those next dark paintings.

{{< row-start >}}
    {{< img col="6" src="blossoms.jpg" alt="Work in progress watercolor painting of cherry blossoms" title="Flower's for Dianne" >}}
{{< row-end >}}

Now for what's next. **["I’m Sorry, It’s Cancer"](/story/im-sorry-its-cancer)** kicked off the series, that will iterate back in time through some of my most darkest moments, for the collection "[Beauty in Darkness](/collections/beauty-in-darkness/)".

My next painting, which is currently being worked on, is **[Returning the Favor](/story/returning-the-favor/)**. Depicting the moment of Izzy's last moments, as I laid her head in to my lap as she was dying. Reflecting a moment almost seven years before, when Izzy first laid her head on my lap, on a day I would die inside.

After that, is working on that reflected moment, **[Broken](/story/broken/)**. It takes us back to the very first hours of December 21st, 2013. With Izzy laying her head on my lap for the first time, as we both mourned the loss of [AJ](/story/final-embrace/), someone we both dearly loved. This would be the very start of my PTSD, that took away who I was for over six years.

After that, I will get a little out of order, and paint the very first moment AJ and I met. The title is still a work in progress, but for now, I'm naming it **"Show Me What You're Looking For"**. This will also act as the beginning painting of my next series.

Then I will return to more lighted and pleasant paintings outside the series for some time, and expand on the story of the building relationship of AJ an I. These romantic works I would hope would be something more comforting to hang in one's home. The series to expand this narrative, will be "**[The Heart's Desire](/collections/the-hearts-desire/)**".

Finally, the most heart wrenching moment for me to think and type about, on December 20th, 2013, is **[Goodbye](/story/goodbye/)**. This painting will conclude the [Beauty in Darkness series](/collections/beauty-in-darkness/) and [The Heart's Desire series](/collections/the-hearts-desire/). It will likely my most uncomfortable and tragic painting to look at, for both myself and others. It will show you a nightmare I had to relive over and over, for many years, until it's grip of my psych finally started to loosen.

These final pieces in the **[Beauty in Darkness series](/collections/beauty-in-darkness/)** all are accompanied by writings that were done years before I started painting. Altered slightly for release, they were each written during a process of healing and trauma therapy. You may read those writings ahead of time, for each piece.

### The Purpose of the Darkness

Then from there it will be back to more lighted pieces. As I mentioned before, my whole body of work perfectly exemplifies "From Darkness to Light". My work is not meant to focus on the darkness as a whole, but to show the depth of the darkness, to contrast the more lighted moments, so they can be much more appreciated. Hope can only come out of a necessity for it. If it wasn't for the loss, and the PTSD, I would not be who I am today. And even literally from a technical standpoint, my realism work heavily uses light and darkness to contrast the depth, in sometimes even a Chiaroscuro way.

My work will hopefully one day will help others that may be in a dark place of their own, and show them it's only temporary.
