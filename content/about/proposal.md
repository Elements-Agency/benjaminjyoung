**Medium:** Primarily Representational Watercolor (with select transition works in Oil on Canvas)  
**Style:** Figurative Narrative Realism / Chiaroscuro  
**Scope:** 16 Chronological Works  

---

## Project Overview

*Beauty in Darkness* is an in-progress, sixteen-piece body of representational figurative narrative paintings that confronts grief, complex trauma, post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), and the slow, unvarnished work of survival. Executed in a dramatic chiaroscuro style, the series traces a chronological arc through the most defining and devastating chapters of my life—from childhood abuse and sudden bereavement to betrayal, institutional devastation, and a life-altering medical diagnosis.

Rather than treating trauma as a single isolated event, *Beauty in Darkness* examines PTSD as an accumulation: the quiet, compounding weight of earlier wounds colliding with catastrophic loss. By rendering these deeply personal moments through classical light-and-shadow storytelling, the exhibition invites viewers to sit inside the tension between unbearable grief and the stubborn, luminous persistence of human grace.

---

## Technical Innovation: Pushing Watercolor into the Dark

While two transitional works in this cycle are executed in oil—honoring pivotal shifts in my artistic development—the core of *Beauty in Darkness* is built in **watercolor**, pushed to a level of layered darkness, tonal density, and figurative realism rarely attempted in the medium.

Watercolor is traditionally celebrated for translucency, airy washes, and high-key luminosity. In this series, I subvert those expectations by building dozens of disciplined, transparent glazes to achieve velvet, oil-like depths of shadow while preserving the luminous glow of the paper beneath. Using strict chiaroscuro lighting, figures emerge out of near-total darkness—allowing light to function not merely as an optical effect, but as a psychological lifeline. Every highlight on skin, fabric, or a tear-streaked face becomes an act of survival carved out of shadow.

## The Chronological Narrative & Proposed Works

Arranged in the exact sequence in which these events occurred, the sixteen paintings form a visual memoir—moving from early conditioning, through transcendent love and sudden loss, into the psychological aftermath of PTSD and the ongoing search for healing.

### Act I: Foundations & Ruptures

1. **Children Should Be Seen But Not Heard** *(Watercolor)*  
   The series opens in childhood, depicting a moment of physical abuse at the hands of my maternal grandfather—an act of senseless scapegoating that established an early blueprint of fear and powerlessness. This foundational piece introduces a central thesis of the exhibition: PTSD rarely begins with a single crisis; it is built layer by layer across a lifetime until a breaking point is reached.

2. **I'm Going To Be A Father** *(Watercolor)*  
   Rendered in stark, claustrophobic shadow, this work captures the shock and disorientation of learning I would become a teenage father—the sudden realization that the future I had planned for myself had vanished overnight. Paired in dialogue with a later painting in the series, it examines how the same life-altering news can feel like an emotional collapse when forced upon you without love or choice.

---

### Act II: Love, Joy, and Sudden Catastrophe

3. **AJ** *(Watercolor)*  
   Light breaks into the narrative for the first time in a portrait capturing the exact moment I met my late fiancée, AJ—a study in recognition, warmth, and love at first sight.

4. **I'm Going To Be A Father #2** *(Watercolor)*  
   Serving as an emotional counterpoint to the second painting in the series, this piece depicts the moment AJ shared that we were expecting a child together. Bathed in warm, intimate illumination, the figure is overwhelmed not by dread, but by tears of pure joy—demonstrating how love transforms fear into devotion.

5. **Goodbye** *(Watercolor)*  
   The emotional center of gravity of the exhibition. Following the car crash that took AJ's life, this composition captures the raw, agonizing moment of holding her lifeless body tightly and weeping—a portrait of grief at its most visceral and unguarded.

6. **Broken** *(Watercolor)*  
   Set in the cold, gray light of early morning just hours after AJ's death, I sit shell-shocked on a bathroom floor with blood still on my shirt. In the quiet stillness of that devastation, Izzy—the Border Collie AJ and I had adopted together—offers her very first gesture of comfort, resting her head gently in my lap.

---

### Act III: The Architecture of Trauma

7. **Night Terror #1** *(Watercolor)*  
   The first of a triptych exploring the subconscious aftermath of sudden loss. In a recurring dream, AJ falls backward into an abyss of darkness, crying out and reaching toward me as I remain powerless to pull her back.

8. **Night Terror #2** *(Watercolor)*  
   A dreamscape of helplessness and violation, depicting menacing figures pulling AJ away from me while I struggle in vain to intervene.

9. **Night Terror #3** *(Watercolor)*  
   Quieter yet equally devastating, this third dream study portrays AJ simply walking out through a doorway, leaving me behind—capturing the lingering guilt and abandonment trauma that haunt waking and sleeping hours alike.

---

### Act IV: Reckoning, Betrayal, and Endurance

10. **[Hallelujah](/story/hallelujah)** *(Oil on Canvas, Completed)*  
    Marking my first major work in oil, this piece documents a turning point: Gertrude, a woman who ironically shares the same last name as my late AJ, stepping into my life to intimately care for me during the onset of severe PTSD, helping guide me toward professional treatment so that trauma would no longer dictate my survival.

11. **Glückschmerz von Gertrude** *(Watercolor, Work In Progress)*  
    Following the bitter collapse of our relationship, due to my own emotional awareness at the time, this psychological portrait captures Gertrude in quiet, calculating contemplation—a study in malice and resentment, portraying someone consumed by spite and actively plotting the ruin of another's life.

12. **Returning the Favor** *(Watercolor, Work In Progress)*  
    A poignant visual echo of *Broken*. Years after Izzy laid her head in my lap to anchor me through the worst morning of my life, she rests her head in my lap once more as she takes her final breaths—closing a circle of loyalty, companionship, and reciprocal grace.

13. **Lies** *(Watercolor)*  
    A tense, high-contrast study of the exact instant one realizes their life is about to be dismantled—the moment someone in power dismisses the truth as fabrication, triggering a wrongful, catastrophic collapse of freedom and reputation. The composition focuses on the psychological shock of disbelief, systemic betrayal, and sudden erasure.

14. **Silver Ballet** *(Oil on Canvas, Work In Progress)*  
    Executed on a four-by-four-foot canvas—and likely my final major oil painting—this work depicts an elderly woman dancing ballet. Inspired by the deep bonds I formed while working in a nursing home and the sorrow of watching those residents pass away over the years, the painting honors dignity, memory, and grace in the twilight of life.

15. **Coward** *(Watercolor)*  
    Set within the deep shadows of a forest, this narrative piece portrays the [man who murdered my cousin](https://crimesolverscentral.com/homicide/182563) cowering behind a tree in fear—confronting the hollowness of violence and the lingering shadow cast over surviving families.

16. **[I'm Sorry, It's Cancer](/story/im-sorry-its-cancer)** *(Watercolor, Completed)*  
    The final work in the sequence is a stark, unflinching self-portrait capturing the quiet stillness of receiving a cancer diagnosis. Returning to the intimate chiaroscuro that defines the entire series, the painting leaves the viewer not in despair, but in radical honesty—looking directly into the dark while refusing to turn away from the light that remains.

## Impact & Need for Funding

*Beauty in Darkness* is both a technical milestone in contemporary realist watercolor and a deeply human testament to resilience. Because each watercolor demands weeks of painstaking multi-layered glazing, archival materials, custom framing, and uninterrupted studio focus, grant support is essential to bring the remaining works to completion and prepare the full sixteen-piece exhibition for public display.

Funding will directly support:
* **Archival Materials & Framing:** Heavyweight 100% cotton watercolor paper, professional artist-grade pigments, and museum-standard framing to protect delicate dark glazes.
* **Studio Time & Production:** Dedicated studio hours required to execute complex figurative compositions and multi-layer chiaroscuro studies.
* **Exhibition Readiness:** High-resolution documentation, didactic wall texts, and framing preparation for solo gallery and museum submissions.

Through *Beauty in Darkness*, my goal is to create an exhibition space where viewers who have carried silent grief, trauma, or loss feel recognized—proving that even in the deepest shadow, beauty and meaning can still be forged.
