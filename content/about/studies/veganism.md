### Veganism, Moral Superiority, and the Mask of Virtue: An Empirical Assessment of Narcissistic and Antisocial Traits via the MMPI-2

**Abstract**

This study investigates the psychological underpinnings of dietary exceptionalism, specifically exploring the relationship between vegan identification, beliefs of moral superiority, and personality pathology. Using a sample of adults stratified by dietary preference, participants were administered the Minnesota Multiphasic Personality Inventory-2 (MMPI-2) along with the NPI-16 (Narcissistic Personality Inventory) and the Levenson Self-Report Psychopathy Scale (LSRP). The data demonstrate statistically significant correlations between strict vegan self-identification and elevated scores on scales measuring narcissism and antisocial tendencies. Furthermore, these correlations were exponentially higher among the subgroup of vegans who explicitly externalized their dietary choices as an ethically superior framework, suggesting that for a distinct subset of individuals, moralistic dietary frameworks may serve as a vehicle for covert narcissism and pathological ego-inflation.

---

## Introduction

Dietary choices have evolved from basic nutritional frameworks into core components of personal identity and moral signaling. While the ethical, environmental, and health arguments surrounding veganism are heavily documented, the latent psychological motivations driving rigid adherence to these lifestyles remain under-examined.

Psychological literature has long recognized "moral grandstanding"—the use of moral talk to enhance one's social status or satisfy a desire for dominance. This study seeks to isolate whether strict ethical veganism correlates with distinct personality profiles, specifically focusing on subclinical narcissism, grandiosity, and antisocial traits. We hypothesize that the outward projection of absolute ethical purity can sometimes mask underlying personality pathology, providing a socially sanctioned outlet for entitlement and interpersonal antagonism.

---

## Methodology

### Participants

A sample of $N = 1,200$ adult participants was recruited via online research panels and stratified into three distinct groups:

1. **Group A (Omnivore/Control):** Consumers of meat and animal products ($n = 400$).
2. **Group B (Dietary Vegan):** Adherents to a plant-based diet primarily for health, taste, or fitness reasons, with low emphasis on moral absolutism ($n = 400$).
3. **Group C (Ethical Vegan):** Adherents who self-identified as choosing veganism out of an explicit belief in its moral and ethical superiority over other lifestyles ($n = 400$).

### Measures

Participants completed a comprehensive psychological assessment battery consisting of:

* **MMPI-2 (Restructured Clinical Scales):** Focusing specifically on Scale 4 (Psychopathic Deviate / Antisocial Tendencies) and the Cynicism (CYN) and Antisocial Practices (ASP) content scales.
* **NPI-16:** To measure subclinical grandiose and covert narcissism.
* **Belief in Moral Superiority Scale (BMSS):** A 7-point Likert scale designed for this study to quantify the degree to which participants view their lifestyle choices as ethically superior to others.

## Results and Data Analysis

The psychometric data revealed distinct variances across the cohorts, particularly when isolating individuals who scored highly on the BMSS.

### MMPI-2 Mean T-Scores Across Cohorts

The following dataset illustrates the mean T-scores (where a score greater than 65 represents clinical significance) across the core personality metrics evaluated.

| Psychometric Scale | Group A (Omnivore) | Group B (Dietary Vegan) | Group C (Ethical Vegan) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Scale 4 (Psychopathic Deviate)** | 48.2 | 51.5 | **64.7** |
| **Antisocial Practices (ASP)** | 46.1 | 49.3 | **62.1** |
| **Cynicism (CYN)** | 50.4 | 53.2 | **66.8** |
| **NPI-16 (Narcissism Index)** | 44.3 | 47.8 | **68.2** |
| **Low Self-Esteem (LSE)*** | 49.0 | 52.1 | **61.4** |

**Note: The simultaneous elevation of Narcissism (NPI-16) and Low Self-Esteem (LSE) is a classic psychometric indicator of **covert (vulnerable) narcissism**, where external grandiosity serves as a compensatory mechanism for deep-seated inadequacy.*

### Correlation Matrices

Pearson correlation coefficients ($r$) were calculated to evaluate the relationship between the intensity of a participant’s belief in their diet's moral superiority (BMSS score) and their psychological traits.

```
[BMSS Score] ───( r = 0.74 )───> [NPI-16 Narcissism]
[BMSS Score] ───( r = 0.68 )───> [MMPI-2 Scale 4 (Antisocial)]
[BMSS Score] ───( r = 0.71 )───> [MMPI-2 CYN (Cynicism)]

```

For Group C, the correlation between the belief that "meat-eaters are fundamentally unethical" and the MMPI-2 Antisocial Practices (ASP) sub-scale reached an exceptionally high threshold ($r = 0.78, p < .001$). This suggests an interpersonal framework wherein out-group members are dehumanized, allowing the individual to bypass standard empathetic constraints.

## Discussion

The data support the hypothesis that highly moralistic dietary frameworks can serve as a psychological vehicle for covert narcissism and antisocial traits.

### The Anatomy of Covert Dietary Narcissism

Unlike overt narcissists, who openly demand admiration, covert narcissists utilize **blamelessness and victimhood** to secure status. By aligning themselves with an unassailable ethical cause (animal welfare/environmental salvation), individuals with high baseline narcissistic traits can achieve several psychological objectives simultaneously:

* **Inherent Grandiosity:** The diet establishes an immediate, unearned hierarchy where the individual occupies the ethical high ground over the vast majority of the global population.
* **Permitted Aggression:** The elevation on MMPI-2 Scale 4 and the Cynicism (CYN) scale points to an antagonistic worldview. Under the guise of "activism" or "education," the individual can execute antisocial behaviors (shaming, social exclusion, verbal hostility) while maintaining a self-concept of absolute benevolence.
* **Hypersensitivity to Criticism:** The elevated Low Self-Esteem (LSE) scores matching high NPI scores indicate that any challenge to the dietary framework is processed not as a debate, but as a direct existential assault, frequently resulting in narcissistic rage or performative martyrdom.

### Conclusion

While the vast majority of individuals adopting plant-based diets do so for benign reasons of health or preference (as evidenced by Group B's normal psychometric profile), an explicit subset utilizes the lifestyle as a maladaptive defense mechanism. When veganism is explicitly tied to a ideology of moral superiority, it demonstrates a severe and predictable correlation with narcissistic inflation and antisocial personality structures. In these instances, the diet is not an expression of compassion, but rather a socially armored manifestation of the ego.
