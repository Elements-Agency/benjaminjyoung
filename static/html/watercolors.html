The Artist Behind the Brush

Quiet Honesty in Drybrush Watercolor & Mineral Pigments

Benjamin J. Young’s path to fine art began not in an academic atelier, but in the quiet reckoning that followed stepping away from a demanding corporate career and confronting a life-altering cancer diagnosis. Turning toward paper and pigment as a discipline of survival and stillness, Young developed an intimate, labor-intensive approach to drybrush watercolor on 100% cotton paper, alongside work in oil and egg tempera.

Working primarily with a restrained mineral and Zorn-inspired palette—ivory black, burnt umber, yellow ochre, Venetian red, and Ultramarine blue—Young strips away superfluous color to heighten emotional weight and chiaroscuro contrast. Every layer of drybrush pigment is built slowly, allowing the tooth of the cotton rag to breathe through shadow and muted light.

Within his first three years of exhibiting, Young has emerged as one of the fastest-growing representational realists in the United States. His works have been featured in over 30 juried and invitational exhibitions nationwide, earned multiple honors for portraiture and narrative realism, and found permanent homes with more than 1,200 collectors across over 20 countries.
